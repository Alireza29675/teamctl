TeamCTL is an open-source platform for autonomous teams of agents. The identity pairs a compact character logo with a larger illustration of two teammates building together.

## The head is the logo

<img src="/brand/teamctl-head.svg" alt="TeamCTL's Signal-blue hooded head with a tilted Chalk face and a small smile" width="160" height="160" />

Use the head for browser tabs, navigation, avatars and compact branding. The editable SVG has flat colours, a transparent background and no embedded image or font.

[SVG master](/brand/teamctl-head.svg) · [Transparent PNG](/brand/teamctl-head.png) · [Favicon ICO](/favicon.ico) · [32px favicon](/brand/favicon-32.png)

Keep the supplied square canvas, proportions and face tilt. Its padding protects the hood at small sizes. In larger layouts, leave at least one eye-width around the silhouette. Use a plain Chalk or Graphite background.

## Colours and type

| Colour | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signal | `#2F5EFF` | Hood, links and primary actions. |
| Chalk | `#F7F7F2` | Face and light surfaces. |
| Graphite | `#191B1A` | Outline, eyes, smile and text. |
| Mist | `#E6E7E1` | Quiet borders and supporting surfaces. |

Use Manrope for interface text and headings, with monospace for commands and configuration. Keep paragraphs short, headings descriptive and generous space between sections.

## Voice

**Create autonomous teams of agents.**

Define the roles, structure and conversations. Run your team in the background, and share the setup so others can use it or make it their own.

Explain the platform through contrasting configurations: two research partners, a lead with specialists, or several project teams. Describe what people can create and configure, then show how to start it.

Write **TeamCTL** in prose and `teamctl` in commands, package names and paths. Use concrete verbs: define, connect, run, inspect, share and adapt. Use descriptive section headings that name the behavior or configuration: “Team structures,” “Run agents in the background” and “Define roles and communication.” Keep claims tied to configured behavior, tools and permissions.

Background work requires a running host, active sessions and provider access. A starter definition shows one possible team; it does not promise a guaranteed result.
